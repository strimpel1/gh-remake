1. (0–8.5) Your health says one hundred. But the game never wrote down a one and two zeros. So what did it store?
2. (8.5–17.6) Dive into the game's memory and you find rows of this: pairs of digits and letters. One pair is your health.
3. (17.6–27.2) This one. Six-four. Not sixty-four: this is hexadecimal, base sixteen, and it means exactly one hundred.
4. (27.2–35.3) Underneath, it is one byte: eight bits, each a zero or a one. That is all the machine really has.
5. (35.3–45.5) Now split the eight bits into two groups of four. The first group is worth six. The second is worth four.
6. (45.5–55.4) Four bits can make sixteen patterns, so you need sixteen digits. Zero to nine, then A through F for ten to fifteen.
7. (55.4–65.1) Fill every bit with a one and both digits become F. F-F is two hundred fifty-five, the most one byte can hold.
8. (65.1–72.9) So write F-F into that byte, climb back out, and your health reads two hundred fifty-five.
9. (72.9–80.4) Hex is not a different number. It is the same bits, four at a time.
